The Big Story · The Strait Is The Prize

IRAN JUST TOLD THE WORLD WHAT IT IS NEGOTIATING FOR: GHALIBAF SAYS HORMUZ "WILL NEVER RETURN" TO ITS PRE WAR STATUS, CLAIMS AN UNDERSTANDING WITH OMAN TO JOINTLY OVERSEE THE STRAIT AND DECLARES UNCLOS INAPPLICABLE, WHILE ARAGHCHI DANGLES A REOPENING "WITHIN TWO DAYS" IF WASHINGTON MEETS THE SEVEN CONDITIONS, AND THE IRGC SAYS THREE TANKERS BURNED ON AN "UNAUTHORISED ROUTE" TO MAKE THE POINT

Parliament Speaker and chief negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf gave Al Jazeera the clearest statement yet of Iran's endgame (Business Standard/ANI, 12 Nov): "the Strait of Hormuz will never return to its pre war status", Tehran and Oman "have reached an understanding on overseeing the waterway", and UNCLOS does not apply because "the regulations within the Strait of Hormuz are governed by the laws of the two littoral states that border it". The seven conditions sent via Qatar: lifting the naval blockade, lifting sanctions, releasing frozen funds, ending hostilities on all fronts, and an explicit announcement that the war has ended; "the ball is now in the American court". His claim that US officials asked Putin to help arrange direct talks is single source, UNVERIFIED. The carrot beside the stick: Araghchi told IRNA the strait could reopen "within two days" if demands are met, while Rezaei called talk of a Qatar meeting a "pure lie" and the US response so far "insufficient and deceptive" (Al Jazeera, 11 Nov). And the enforcement mechanism showed itself the same day: the IRGC said three oil tankers caught fire after striking naval mines while transiting an "unauthorised route" in the eastern strait, with follow up reports of two more tankers hitting mines and four crew injured on a Sinokor VLCC (vessel identities and dates not fully captured, UNVERIFIED). Trump, meanwhile: "positive momentum", new attacks unnecessary, and the war ends "when we want to end it".

Why it matters: this reframes the entire reopening trade. The question is no longer WHEN Hormuz reopens but UNDER WHOSE RULES: Iran is negotiating for a permanent change in the strait's governance, with Oman as co administrator, and it is enforcing "authorised routes" with mines in the meantime. For the desk that means the war risk basis is not a temporary surcharge waiting for a ceasefire, it is pricing a structural regime change in Gulf shipping. The Araghchi "two days" line guarantees headline whipsaw in both directions. And the Oman angle just became the single most commercially interesting sentence of the war: whatever geometry emerges, Muscat is at the centre of it.

War Risk · Mines, Sinkings And A Severe Rating

The UKMTO weekly ledger reads like a war grave registry: eleven ships attacked in and around Hormuz in the week to 6 November and THREE OF THEM SANK, 88 projectile strikes since July, transits running 65% below pre conflict levels, and now the IRGC's mines enforcing "authorised routes"

The structural numbers (Gulf News, updated 10 Nov): UKMTO's weekly report counts 170 outbound and 155 inbound transits, roughly 65% below pre conflict levels, traffic "well below normal but not stalled" and likely undercounted with transponders dark; at least 11 ships attacked in or around the strait in the week ending 6 Nov, of which THREE SANK; 88 projectile strikes recorded since early July; JMIC logged 5 new incidents in its 8 Nov update, 177 since 1 March, threat level SEVERE; CENTCOM says the blockade has redirected 147 vessels and disabled five since July. Onto that ledger, this week's additions: the IRGC's three mined tankers on the "unauthorised route" (plus the reported two more and the Sinokor VLCC's four injured, UNVERIFIED), Tuesday's tanker struck 19nm off Lima (UKMTO Warning 201-26) and the ADNOC L&S vessel, whose identity, damage and casualties ADNOC has still not disclosed (gCaptain, 10 Nov), an explicit null now two days old. The standing counts hold: JMIC daily transits "single digits to low double digits" vs ~138/day in 2025, 91 US facilitated transits over 5 to 7 Nov, ~162 ships anchored off Fujairah and Khor Fakkan.

Why it matters: three sinkings in one week is a step change in severity that the diplomatic tape has entirely obscured, and mines are a different underwriting animal from drones: they do not deescalate when the shooter stands down, they wait. The "authorised route" framing means the IRGC is now operating a permission system for the world's most important oil artery, which is Ghalibaf's "never return to pre war status" made physical. Nothing in this tape supports cutting war risk from quotes; everything in it supports the same day advisory trigger for any UAE flagged or UAE waters incident.
