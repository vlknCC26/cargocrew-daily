The Big Story · Day Fifteen

BOTH DOORS CLOSING: SAUDI'S HORMUZ BYPASS IS SHUT AS THE HOUTHIS THREATEN THE STRAIT

Saturday delivered the pivot the market feared. Saudi Arabia's East West pipeline, the kingdom's only Hormuz workaround, is confirmed suspended after drone strikes; Trump said Iran was "probably" responsible, and Iraq agreed to a joint investigation into launch platforms found near its border. The Houthis, now holding Yemen's entire Red Sea coast, threatened to close the strait and warned Washington off intervention. Hormuz itself runs at roughly six transits a day against an ~85 baseline, with war risk insurance near 40 times normal and 434 vessels holding off berth (tracker data, single source). And Tehran spent Saturday talking down Monday's Oman meeting: seven conditions for reopening, "no signed deal expected". Against all that, two thin threads of hope: no Iranian ballistic fire for a third day (absence of reporting, unverified), and Iran's president met the UAE Crown Prince at the BRICS summit, the first such contact since the war began. Brent closed Friday at $104.61, down 2.8% on the day but up 9% on the week, as the IEA slashed its 2026 demand outlook to the largest contraction since the pandemic (AJ ↗ · ToI ↗ · SL ↗).

Why it matters: with the pipeline shut and Bab al-Mandeb under threat, Gulf outbound logistics is structurally dependent on airfreight for anything time critical, and tomorrow's IATA fuel print prices that reality into every invoice. Reprice today, before the print: 48 to 72 hour validity on spot, fuel indexation in every open tender, surcharge pre advisories to the top forwarders. Being the GSA that warned customers first is cheap goodwill.

Geopolitics · The Corridor Question

Iran sets seven conditions and talks down Monday's Oman meeting

Tasnim says the Iran Oman understanding "does not provide for immediate reopening"; Bahrain refuses to attend any Hormuz meeting until ties are restored; the Khasab twin vessel strike stays unattributed (UKMTO Warning 133-26), the second unclaimed twin strike in Omani waters.

Why it matters: the corridor keeps being promised and keeps not opening, while the "safe" Omani side takes fire. Keep war risk clauses on everything; the BRICS handshake between Iran and the UAE is the channel to watch, not the Oman talks.
Rates · Wednesday's Vote

FedWatch says ~90%, prediction markets say ~80%: watch the dissent count

Three policymakers dissented in favour of a hike back in July; the question Wednesday is not the 25 points but the vote split and the December signal. The IEA's demand contraction call is the new counterweight to the war premium.

Why it matters: a hawkish split with December live keeps the 10 year near 5% through Q4; hold long dated pricing until Wednesday afternoon, then lock what you can.
