The Big Story · The Lull Is Over

DUBAI IN THE CROSSHAIRS AGAIN: US AND IRAN RESUME STRIKES, BRENT BACK ABOVE $90, AND IATA'S JULY DATA SHOW THE GULF LANES STILL BLEEDING

The 31 day lull broke on Sunday when CENTCOM struck two IRGC rocket launchers on Larak Island that it said were preparing to fire sea mines into the strait. Iran answered Monday with ballistic missiles at two bases in Jordan and a state TV claim of a strike on Al Minhad air base in Dubai; the UAE denied any hit but confirmed it engaged an Iranian drone over its territorial waters, the first against a Gulf state in twelve days. Brent rose 2.8% to about $90.5. The Oman corridor framework is in limbo, with no joint statement issued, and Iran's SNSC has published six preconditions no deal has addressed. On the same day IATA's July data landed: global CTK +3.9% (down from June's +8.5%), Middle East carriers +1.7% with capacity up 4.0% and a 44.1% load factor, and the Gulf hub lanes still contracting: Europe to Middle East 16.1% down, Middle East to Asia 14.1% down (KT ↗ · IATA ↗).

Why it matters: customers will be calling today, and the two stories answer each other. The IATA numbers are why MESA rates run +42% YoY: capacity is outrunning demand on Gulf hub lanes and freighters are taking share from belly. The escalation is why last week's 4.3% fuel relief is about to reverse. Lock September space out of Dubai and India to Europe now, before the surcharge resets catch the next fuel print, and have contingency freighter capacity on a ready rate card if routings tighten.
