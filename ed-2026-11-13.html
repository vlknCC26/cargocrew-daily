The Big Story · The Track Wobbles

THE DIPLOMATIC TRACK WOBBLES: BLOCKADE STAYS, ISRAEL PREPARES, FOUR TANKERS BURN

Thursday reversed Wednesday's "positive momentum" point by point. Trump said the US does not need a deal with Iran and will keep the blockade on oil, trade and sanctions in full effect (The New Arab, 12 Nov). Israeli media reported Netanyahu's security cabinet ordered the military to step up preparations for a possible new attack on Iran (Al Jazeera, 12 Nov); Ynet's report of a "shouting match" Trump call is denied by Netanyahu's office, UNVERIFIED. Tehran rejected a US proposal to transfer its 60% enriched uranium abroad and warned that any new war makes every involved base "from West Asia to Europe" a target. Overnight, Iranian accounts said four tankers burned in and east of Hormuz, two supertankers hit on an "unauthorised route" and two mined; no independent confirmation, UNVERIFIED. Brent jumped 3.3% to $95.82 and still heads for a second weekly decline.

Context: the market's question has shifted from "when does the strait reopen" to "does the air war restart." That puts Gulf airspace advisories, next week's Saudi route restorations (flydubai Riyadh and Abha from 17 Nov, Etihad's cancellations to 19 Nov) and every reopening dated commitment back in play, and it explains why oil can rally on headlines yet fall on the week.

Capacity & Rates Outlookeditorial model, not advice

Near term (one to two weeks): rates more likely to rise than fall

The signals point the same way. Capacity: Gulf capacity ~18% below pre war, Europe cut 6% in a week, global belly down 3% with freighters only partly compensating (WorldACD wk44). Demand: peak season with AI hardware flows a named driver, transpacific spot above $8/kg at last year's high. Friction: Shenzhen's APEC week and the 20 Nov port deadline push more volume to air. Cost: jet fuel down 6.6% relieves the input side without, so far, pulling rates down. Absent a ceasefire, the balance favours further firmness into late November.

The swing factor: the war premium can unwind fast

The downside scenario is singular but large: a genuine de escalation (a deal, a strait reopening, restored Saudi and Gulf frequencies) would release parked capacity and unwind the war premium quickly; oil's 16% weekly fall two weeks ago shows the speed. The escalation branch (a new strike wave) cuts capacity further and spikes fuel.
