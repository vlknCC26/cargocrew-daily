The Big Story · The Target Map Widens

THE HOUTHIS GO FOR THE OIL MAP: MISSILES AT TAIF AND YANBU DOWNED, CLAIMS AGAINST ARAMCO AND THE EAST WEST PIPELINE, WHILE RIYADH'S AIRPORT REOPENS AND EMIRATES PUSHES RIYADH TO 8 NOVEMBER

Saturday did not stop at the dawn Riyadh intercept carried yesterday. Around noon the coalition downed two more ballistic missiles heading for Taif and Yanbu (Brig. Gen. Al-Maliki), with danger warnings briefly issued for Jeddah, Yanbu, Taif and Makkah before being lifted; no damage or casualties were reported. Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree then claimed a far wider target list: King Khalid International itself, Aramco in Yanbu, the Rabigh refinery, the East West pipeline, King Fahd International Dammam, Najran airport and Khamis Mushait airbase (claims only, none independently confirmed, UNVERIFIED). Riyadh's KKIA, closed after the attacks, announced it resumes operations today, Sunday 25 Oct: T1 for domestic flights, T2 for international services by national carriers, with passengers told to verify bookings first (airport advisory on X, via ANI). The foreign carriers are not treating that as an all clear: Emirates extended Jeddah and Abha to 29 Oct and its Riyadh resumption is now listed 8 November (Gulf Today, 23 Oct; a correction to yesterday's board, which still carried EK to 25 Oct), Air India and Air India Express suspended Riyadh and Dammam through 7 Nov and Jeddah through 31 Oct, and Qatar Airways has announced nothing since its 24 Oct lapse date passed. A separate report says Aramco ordered its Dhahran HQ evacuated on Thursday (single source, UNVERIFIED). In Washington, Ambassador Barrack mused "Could there be a strike this weekend? Absolutely", against Trump's own stated post midterm timeline (ANI ↗ · GT ↗ · CBS ↗).

Why it matters: Yanbu, Rabigh and the East West pipeline are the Red Sea bypass, the very infrastructure that keeps Saudi barrels moving while Hormuz carries a war premium. Claims against them, even unconfirmed, tell underwriters the bypass is now in the declared target set; a confirmed hit there puts $116.72 Brent closer to the floor than the ceiling. And the reopening pattern is the cargo tell: Riyadh reopens for national carriers while Emirates prices 8 Nov and Air India 7 Nov. The carriers' own dates keep saying November, which is the season length DWC transshipment argument written by the market itself.

War Risk · Weekend Words, Hardening Lines

Iran's military chief vows no US ships in the Gulf "at any time", Washington's own ambassador says a weekend strike is "absolutely" possible, and the State Department rewrites travel advice for 14 countries

The weekend's escalation was verbal but pointed. Iran's armed forces chief of staff Abdollahi: "We will not allow U.S. military ships in the Persian Gulf under any circumstances and at any time." The IRGC spokesman called US threats "largely for media consumption" and vowed a "much larger response" to renewed strikes. On the US side, Ambassador to Turkey Tom Barrack answered "Could there be a strike this weekend? Absolutely", even as Trump repeated at a Florida rally that "We may do another little Epic Fury, or they'll agree." The State Department updated travel advisories for 14 Middle East countries on 24 Oct, citing Houthi aggression and Iranian plots against Americans. No follow up to the 23 Oct Istanbul R4 plus E3 session was found through 25 Oct. One opening: Iran's ambassador to Islamabad said Tehran remains open to talks under the June interim deal and would "study the Mecca Agreement, and if it is good, we will support it."

Why it matters: nine days to the midterms, and the signal is split at the top of the US government itself; planners cannot price "after 3 Nov" as safe when the ambassador says "absolutely" about this weekend. The 14 advisory rewrite moves crew planning, war risk riders and corporate travel policy immediately, whatever the missiles do.
