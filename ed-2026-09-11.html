The Big Story · The Twin Squeeze

BRENT HOLDS $108 AS HORMUZ RUNS AT 9% OF NORMAL, AND TODAY'S CPI DECIDES THE HIKE

Everything your customers will feel next week converges today. Brent traded toward $108 into Friday after touching nearly $110 Thursday, on pace for a roughly 12% weekly gain, the largest since mid July, while Saudi Arabia reported its lowest oil production since 1990. Hormuz commodity transits are running at 11 to 12 a day against a ~130 pre war baseline, about 9% of normal, with VLCC charters above $500k a day and Iran now claiming licensing authority over a "restricted maritime zone" beyond the strait: a structural threat to Gulf ocean freight, not an episodic one. And at 16:30 Dubai time the August CPI lands, the last inflation read before Tuesday's FOMC, with hike odds near 70% (from 44% a week ago), the 10 year at 5% for the first time since October 2023, and Thursday's PPI in line at +0.4%. Wednesday's Sirik, Minab and Qeshm strikes remain unclaimed and unconfirmed by CENTCOM; the IRGC claims attacks on ten ships; no incident materialised at Kuwaiti or Bahraini ports (GEF ↗ · SG ↗ · JP ↗).

Why it matters: Monday's IATA print will capture this week's oil run and trigger surcharge resets across principals within days. Today: issue rate validity warnings to forwarder customers, cap quote validity at seven days maximum (72 hours on Gulf lanes), insert fuel adjustment clauses in every Q4 tender, and pre agree surcharge pass through mechanics with principals. The GSA that communicates the reset first keeps the freight. Then reprice everything after 16:30 on known ground.
