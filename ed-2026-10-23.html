The Big Story · Reading the Evacuations

WASHINGTON EMPTIES ITS EMBASSIES, ARAMCO EMPTIES ITS HEADQUARTERS: THE SIGNALS POINT TO THE POST MIDTERM WINDOW

The war's most telling moves on Thursday were departures, not strikes. An AFP diplomatic source says Washington ordered all US embassy staff and their families out of Saudi Arabia, with families of embassy staff also ordered out of the UAE, Jordan, Qatar and Bahrain (22 Oct; the "all staff" level is UNVERIFIED, the public State Department order of 21 Oct covers non emergency personnel). The same day, Aramco told essential staff at its Dhahran headquarters to evacuate and work from home, reportedly the first such evacuation of the war. ABC News (22 Oct) corroborated the NYT report with two Israeli sources: US and Israeli planners have drawn up renewed joint strikes "in the coming weeks", almost certainly after the 3 Nov midterms, targeting petrochemical and nuclear sites, with the White House stating there will be no attack on Iran before the elections and Israeli sources naming regime change as the top goal. Trump said "I think we're not negotiating", then accused Iran of "making up fake hits" on ships to lift oil prices before the midterms. The toll from the 21 Oct Dammam airport strike was revised up to 2 killed (one Saudi, one Indian national), the Houthis claimed 48 hours of attacks on Dammam, Abha, the Riyadh defence ministry and Aramco facilities in Yanbu (claim only, no confirmed Yanbu damage), and the Saudi bourse fell 2.5%. Diplomacy is not dead: the first R4 plus E3 meeting (UK, France, Germany with Pakistan, Turkiye, Saudi Arabia, Egypt) convenes in Istanbul today with Hormuz and the Houthi war on the agenda. Brent answered the week with a ~8% rise to $113.61 intraday Friday; WTI sits back at ~$100 (GN ↗ · AJ ↗ · Dawn ↗ · TE ↗).

Why it matters: evacuation orders are the cheapest reliable signal in this war: they move before strikes do, and this set covers the UAE itself. A corroborated post midterm strike plan gives the market a date window (after 3 Nov) to price, which means war risk premiums, fuel and insurance all have a calendar now. Sell against that calendar: capacity locked before November is worth more than capacity shopped during it, and every Gulf lane quote needs its force majeure and war risk language current this week, not next.

War Risk · The Two Clocks

Istanbul hosts the first R4 plus E3 meeting today as Pakistan and Qatar push a sanctions relief package; the IRGC threatens US bases over convoy escorts

Europe's three FMs meet Pakistan, Turkiye, Saudi Arabia and Egypt in Istanbul (23 Oct) with Hormuz navigation and the Houthi campaign on the agenda. Samaa TV, citing the WSJ, says Pakistan and Qatar are pushing a package of sanctions relief plus access to frozen assets (UNVERIFIED). Iran's seven day Hormuz reopening plan remains on the table. Against that: the IRGC threatened strikes on US bases across the region if convoy escorts continue, claimed hits on 4 vessels plus 3 mine strikes (unsubstantiated, consistent with JMIC's standing assessment), and a ToI headline says the IDF chief sees no operational need to strike Iran before Israel's election (UNVERIFIED).

Why it matters: two clocks now run in public: a diplomatic one ending in a deal that reopens Hormuz in a week, and a military one starting after 3 Nov. The spread between those outcomes is the widest pricing uncertainty of the quarter. Quote short, hedge fuel, and keep an options view on both.

Trade · 18 Days to the Rare Earth Cliff

No renewal in sight for the rare earth suspension lapsing 10 Nov; Trump and Xi expected at APEC Shenzhen as the Section 338 class action awaits a hearing date

Reporting on 20 Oct says Beijing is offering only case by case flexibility, the 28 Sept truce extension (to 10 Jan 2027) did not name the rare earth suspension, and the April 2025 licensing regime for seven heavy rare earths stays in force; EU officials say general licences have begun issuing (UNVERIFIED compilation). Trump and Xi are expected to meet at APEC Shenzhen in November, with negotiators trying to close deals that could slip to a December G20 (via SCMP, UNVERIFIED). On Canada, the Gellert class action (filed 20 Oct, CIT) runs four counts including a violated 30 day waiting period, and seeks a class of all Section 338 duty payers since 22 Aug; no hearing date yet. From 1 Nov the EU adds a €2 small parcel handling fee on top of July's €3 customs surcharge.

Why it matters: the 10 Nov lapse now sits inside the same fortnight as the midterms, APEC and the FOMC aftermath. If licensing snaps back, AI and semiconductor airfreight, the cargo carrying this peak, reprices first. The EU parcel fee is a dated, certain hit to China to Europe e-commerce tonnage: adjust lane forecasts now.
