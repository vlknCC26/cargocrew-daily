The Big Story · Day Four

IRAN STRIKES AT DUBAI'S DOORSTEP, WASHINGTON DELIBERATELY HOLDS FIRE, AND THE DELAY CASCADE EASES

Iran's army says it fired missiles and drones early Thursday at Al Minhad Air Base in Dubai and Ahmed Al Jaber in Kuwait, claiming hits on radar, hangars and satcom; no host government reported damage or casualties, and the UAE has previously called claims of a Minhad hit false (treat the damage claims as unverified). No fourth US wave came, and Reuters reporting explains why: the White House intends to sit in a quiet, economic pressure phase until the November midterms, with deployments extended into 2027. Meanwhile the ramp improved: The National counted fewer disrupted services than Wednesday, with Etihad delays trimmed and only two cancellations across DXB (flydubai ex Entebbe, Kenya Airways). Brent consolidated near $96.8 after spiking toward $98, and only about 6 commodity vessels transited Hormuz on 2 September against a 10 day average near 13 (MEE ↗ · National ↗ · TNR ↗).

Why it matters: the pattern that emerges is a prolonged, elevated risk but flyable plateau, not a spiral. Washington is holding, Iran is firing for headlines not effect, and the airports are recovering. That is a planning window: Q4 capacity, BSA renewals and fuel surcharge indexation can be committed this week with more confidence than at any point since Monday, while competitors stay frozen. Move before this afternoon's US jobs number adds fresh volatility.
