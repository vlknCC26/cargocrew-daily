The Big Story · The Jobs Shock

PAYROLLS TRIPLE THE FORECAST AT +162K: HIKE ODDS SNAP BACK, THE DOLLAR FIRMS, AND BRENT FALLS EVEN ON DAY FIVE

The soft whisper was wrong. August nonfarm payrolls came in at +162,000 against a consensus near +55,000, July's negative print was revised away to +21,000, unemployment held at 4.1% and wages stayed tame at 3.1% YoY. September hike odds snapped back to 62 to 65% from the 50% they touched on Waller's dovish remarks just hours earlier; Capital Economics said even the most committed dove "would struggle to find anything" to justify holding. The 10 year rose to 4.78%, the dollar firmed, gold still closed higher at $4,513, and, notably for cargo, Brent fell to $95.04 despite day five of the Hormuz standoff, as no new strikes materialized Friday and OPEC+ signalled steady policy. In the Gulf, disruption continued without closures: Air France stayed off Dubai through today, Air Arabia cancelled Kuwait and Bahrain rotations, and Hormuz transits ran in single digits (Inv ↗ · TS ↗ · GN ↗).

Why it matters: US demand is stronger than feared, which is constructive for transpacific and consumer air freight into Q4, but dearer dollars and a 4.78% Treasury keep shippers financing lean inventories, which favours just-in-time airfreight over stockpiling. Meanwhile the data blackout deepens: BAI is nearly three weeks dark and wk35 is still missing, so CargoCrew's own booking data is temporarily better than the market's. Use it: canvass forwarders on next week's block space at current levels before any index reprint resets expectations.
