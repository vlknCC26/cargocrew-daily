The Big Story · Day 182

NO WEEKEND BREAKTHROUGH ON HORMUZ, AND THE FINANCIAL WAR WIDENS TO DUBAI'S BANKS

The Friday reopening rumors died on Iran's terms: Supreme National Security Council secretary Rezaei named four conditions (end of the war, lifting of the US blockade of Iranian ports, war damage compensation, sanctions removal) while Trump countered that the strait is already "open" and there is "no urgency" to talk. The live diplomatic track is narrower: an Iran to Oman phased framework for a temporary navigation corridor and Iran to Qatar mine clearing talks. Meanwhile the Banque Misr detail is now verified: FinCEN's formal proposal to sever the bank's UAE branches from US correspondent accounts is published, part of a wave one report sizes at $1.8bn of UAE banking action (single source) (AJ ↗ · FinCEN ↗).

Why it matters: nobody in the market can move Hormuz, but everyone must act on banking channel risk this week. UAE trade finance, carrier remittances and forwarder payment chains all run through the correspondent accounts Washington is now squeezing. Today's move: brief carrier partners on CargoCrew's clean banking and compliance screening, and make that brief a sales differentiator. The crisis pricing on air and sea-air holds while the strait stays shut.
