The Big Story · The Cliff That Didn't Happen

THE RARE EARTH CLIFF WAS DEFUSED WITH ONE DAY TO SPARE: MOFCOM FORMALLY EXTENDED THE EXPORT CONTROL SUSPENSION MONDAY, CHINA PAUSED ITS 24% TARIFF, WASHINGTON SUSPENDED THE SECTION 301 MARITIME ACTION AND EXTENDED 178 EXCLUSIONS, AND THE WHOLE TRUCE NOW RUNS TO A NEW CLIFF ON 10 JANUARY 2027, ALL WHILE BRENT COLLAPSED TO $91 AND JET FUEL PRINTED ITS BIGGEST WEEKLY FALL OF THE WAR

Friday's base case (lapse Tuesday, October 2025 licensing regime returns) is formally dead: on Monday 9 November MOFCOM announced the extension of its rare earth export control suspension, and China's finance ministry suspended the 24% tariff on US goods for two months while keeping the 10% fee; the US side moved simultaneously, with a USTR Federal Register filing suspending the Section 301 maritime and logistics action (the shipping fees) and extending 178 Section 301 exclusions (The National, 9 Nov; Reuters via US News and The Star, 9 Nov). Bessent, whose "the truce covers it" claim the desk carried as one sided on Friday, turns out to have been right in substance and early on paper: Beijing has now published, which is what was missing. The precision matters: this is a two month extension formalising the truce to 10 January 2027, not a resolution, so the October 2025 regime (including the 0.1% extraterritorial content rule) is parked, not abolished, and the new cliff lands in the first full week of January, right in the post peak softness. The macro tape amplified it: Brent fell 5.0% Monday toward $93.38 and traded ~$91.6 Tuesday, below $92 for the first time since late August (last week down 13.3%), and the IATA monitor finally printed the crash, global jet fuel $178.95/bbl, down 6.6% WoW, the first material fuel cost relief since the war began (Reuters ↗ · TE ↗ · IATA ↗).

Why it matters: the desk's weekend rare earth advisory said "until MOFCOM publishes, Tuesday is a lapse". MOFCOM published. The follow up note writes itself and must go out TODAY while customers are still holding Friday's warning: the lapse is off, pre positioned inventory is not wasted (the January cliff gives it a second life), and the Section 301 maritime suspension is a real cost subtraction on transpacific logistics. Separately, the fuel print starts the surcharge conversation every GSSA has been waiting nine months to have: the first print to capture the crash is on the board, the next one captures $91 crude. Quote accordingly, and in writing.
