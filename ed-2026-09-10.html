The Big Story · Day Ten

STRIKES REACH THE STRAIT'S FRONT PORCH: PROJECTILES HIT IRAN'S HORMUZ COAST AS BRENT TOPS $100

Overnight, Iranian media reported "hostile projectiles" striking multiple areas of Sirik, with explosions heard across Minab County and Qeshm Island: the coastline that commands the Strait itself, directly opposite Bandar Abbas (Iranian media only, no CENTCOM confirmation at press time). It lands hours after Brent broke $100 for the first time since 23 July ($100.45 intraday; PVM: supply will not align with demand "unless the Strait of Hormuz re-opens"), the IAEA board referred Iran to the UN Security Council for the first time in two decades over blocked inspections, and Trump said talks "could happen" while predicting the war "will end immediately after the US November midterm elections". No attack materialised at Kuwaiti or Bahraini ports after Tuesday's IRGC evacuation warning, but the threat stands, the IRGC has declared new restricted zones from Chabahar into the Gulf of Oman, and tracker data puts Hormuz commodity transits near 90% below pre war norms with VLCC charters above $500k a day. The Oman corridor stays promised, still unopened (AJ ↗ · Rigzone ↗).

Why it matters: the whole cost stack is now one story: crude, jet fuel, insurance, Gulf routings and the Fed decision move together, and tomorrow's CPI prices it. If the Sirik strikes are confirmed, the fighting sits roughly 150km from DXB approach corridors over the Gulf of Oman: watch for any airspace notice. Hold 72 hour validities, keep war risk clauses on every charter and BSA, and treat Monday's fuel print as capturing $100 Brent.
