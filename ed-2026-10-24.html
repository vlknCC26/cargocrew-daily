The Big Story · The Weekend Test Failed

THE SAUDI RESUMPTIONS DID NOT HAPPEN: FLYDUBAI EXTENDS TO 29 OCTOBER, ETIHAD TO 5 NOVEMBER, AND A MISSILE AT RIYADH OPENS SATURDAY

The 24 to 25 Oct restart window this board was watching closed without a restart. flydubai extended its Riyadh, Dammam and Abha cancellations to 29 Oct (announced 23 Oct), Etihad is now listed out to 5 Nov (The National, 23 Oct; Gulf News same day still carried 24 Oct, the later listing is treated as an extension), Akasa pushed Jeddah to 10 Nov and Riyadh to 30 Nov, and Qatar Airways' suspension, due to lapse 24 Oct, has produced no resumption announcement; QR's own travel alerts page still shows Riyadh and Dammam suspended with no restart date. Emirates remains listed to 25 Oct. The war kept arguing the airlines' case: early Saturday the Saudi led coalition intercepted a Houthi ballistic missile over Riyadh (two explosions heard, no casualties, AFP) and destroyed a drone over the Eastern Province, while Friday the IRGC claimed two laden supertankers struck naval mines in Hormuz, over 4.5m barrels combined, crews abandoning ship (claim only, UNVERIFIED, no independent confirmation; UKMTO separately confirmed a tanker hit by an unknown projectile off Limah on 22 Oct). Brent answered with its highest close since 29 April, $116.72, up 6.2% on the week (settle print single source, direction corroborated); WTI settled $103.27. Diplomacy delivered process, not substance: the first R4 plus E3 meeting in Istanbul ended with agreement to keep meeting, solidarity with Riyadh, and no communiqué, no sanctions relief package, and no movement on Iran's seven day Hormuz plan, while Reuters sources say Iran has drawn up plans to target Gulf undersea internet cables if strikes resume (UNVERIFIED) (GN ↗ · Natl ↗ · Dawn ↗ · AFP ↗).

Why it matters: every pushed resumption date lengthens the window in which Gulf cargo must route around Saudi Arabia, and the carriers just priced that window for you: flydubai says 29 Oct, Etihad says 5 Nov, Akasa says 30 Nov. That is the strongest third party evidence yet for season length DWC transshipment contracts rather than week to week extensions. And a ballistic missile at the capital on the very morning the restarts were due tells underwriters what to do with war risk premiums; quote Gulf lanes accordingly before Monday's repricing, not after it.

War Risk · Process in Istanbul, Threats in the Gulf

The R4 plus E3 meeting ends with an agreement to keep meeting; Iran reportedly readies plans against Gulf undersea cables; Trump muses about "another little epic fury"

Istanbul's first R4 plus E3 session (23 Oct) produced continued engagement in the format, solidarity with Saudi Arabia and condemnation of Houthi attacks, but no communiqué and no movement on sanctions relief or Iran's seven day Hormuz reopening plan; the UK said talks centred on Hormuz navigation, the French FM warned of energy price effects, and the E3 restated readiness to negotiate on the nuclear file. Reuters, via two senior Iranian officials, says Tehran has drawn up plans to target undersea internet cables in the Gulf and delay repairs if US and Israeli attacks resume (UNVERIFIED); TeleGeography notes only a small share of global bandwidth transits Hormuz. Trump told a Florida rally the US could do "another little epic fury" operation and asked whether to "finish it or let it wither"; Gulf News cites NYT reporting that a short, intensive post midterm campaign is planned but not approved. US intel reportedly warns of advanced Iranian plots against American officials (ToI liveblog headline, UNVERIFIED).

Why it matters: the diplomatic clock produced nothing this week, so the military clock after 3 Nov is now the one the market prices. A credible cable threat adds a new tail risk: Gulf connectivity outages would hit booking systems, customs platforms and ports, not just tankers. Worth a line in business continuity plans now.
Money · On Consensus, With a Price Sting

Flash composite PMI prints 57.6 on consensus, but selling prices jump the most since 2022; hold odds 92.8% into a blackout week; the yen firms on Japan's first 2%+ core CPI since December

October's flash PMIs (23 Oct): composite 57.6 (prior 58.4, consensus 57.6), services 57.9, manufacturing 56.0 (above the 55.5 expected), with selling price inflation the fastest in roughly four years as fuel and transport costs pass through. CME FedWatch has hold at 92.8% for the 28 Oct FOMC; Fed speakers are in blackout. USD/JPY closed 156.80 on 23 Oct, the yen firming after Japan core CPI printed 2.10%, the first print above 2% since December; 83% of Bloomberg surveyed economists expect a BoJ hike by December, with the BoJ meeting 29 to 30 Oct.

Why it matters: growth on consensus plus the sharpest selling price jump since 2022 is exactly the mix that keeps the December hike question alive even through a quiet FOMC. For cargo, the PMI price detail is the tell: US firms are passing fuel and transport costs through, which supports the surcharge reset argument with customers. Watch USD/JPY through both central bank meetings; a BoJ surprise moves Japan origin rates.
Trade · 17 Days to the Rare Earth Cliff

Still no renewal signal for the 10 Nov rare earth lapse; the Kremlin floats a trilateral Putin, Trump and Xi meeting at APEC; the EU parcel fee lands in 8 days

No movement through 24 Oct: Beijing still offers case by case flexibility only, the truce extension to 10 Jan 2027 does not name the rare earth suspension, and the April 2025 licensing regime for heavy rare earths stays in force, with general licences reportedly issuing to the EU (compilation UNVERIFIED). New wrinkle: the Kremlin says China is preparing a trilateral Putin, Trump and Xi meeting at the November APEC summit (UNVERIFIED, Kremlin sourced via minor outlets; Axios earlier reported Trump open to such a summit on denuclearization). No formal Trump and Xi confirmation found. The Gellert class action against the Section 338 Canada duties has no hearing date and no government response yet. The EU's €2 per item small parcel handling fee starts 1 Nov as planned, on top of July's €3 customs surcharge.

Why it matters: 17 days out, the base case is now lapse unless APEC delivers, and a trilateral summit (if real) pushes any deal later, not earlier. AI and semiconductor airfreight, the cargo carrying this peak, reprices first on a licensing snapback. The EU fee is dated and certain: China to Europe e-commerce lane forecasts should already carry it.
