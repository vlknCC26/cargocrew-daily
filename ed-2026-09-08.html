The Big Story · The P&L Event

JET FUEL JUMPS 9.0% IN A WEEK TO $171.01: THE WAR PREMIUM ARRIVES ON EVERY INVOICE

The IATA Fuel Monitor finally rolled over and confirmed what every ops desk already felt: global jet fuel rose 9.0% in a week to $171.01 per barrel, the print that turns day eight of the Gulf confrontation into a P&L event for every carrier flying the region. Behind it, the market refused to blink: Brent held $97 into Tuesday ($97.50 intraday Monday) after a third strike in two months on Aramco's Jizan refinery, Hormuz transits fell 28% to 77 vessels last week per Kpler, Iran formalized a maritime "exclusion zone" from the US blockade line toward Hormuz while dangling an Oman managed corridor "days away", and Washington insisted the strait is "fully open". Meanwhile Canada's C$27.6bn counter tariffs went live this morning in three tiers (50% on steel, aluminium, electronics, clothing and cosmetics; 25% on lumber and appliances; 15% on machinery), and one calendar correction matters: US CPI lands Friday, not Wednesday, with September hike odds easing to about 58 to 60% (IATA ↗ · OP ↗ · Blakes ↗).

Why it matters: surcharge resets on principals' tariffs follow this print within days. Audit every open quote, BSA and long validity spot deal issued off the stale $156.85 assumption this morning, shorten validities to 72 hours on Gulf touching lanes, and pre brief the key forwarders that ex DXB and DWC surcharges are moving, so CargoCrew is the first call rather than the bearer of surprise increases. With wk35 six days late, the industry prices this fuel shock blind.
