The Big Story · The War Crosses the Peninsula

DAMMAM AIRPORT STRUCK, SHAYBAH TARGETED, AMERICANS ORDERED OUT: THE WAR REACHES THE GULF COAST

The Houthi air war jumped from the Red Sea side of Saudi Arabia to its Gulf coast on Tuesday. A strike on King Fahd International Airport, Dammam killed one person and injured eight (GACA statement, 21 Oct), the first fatal attack on an Eastern Province airport; Saudi led forces say they downed four drones over the Eastern region. The same day a drone aimed at Aramco's Shaybah oilfield was intercepted, with falling debris killing two, and the US State Department ordered non emergency US government employees and their families to leave Saudi Arabia, the Riyadh embassy warning of further Houthi attacks. Riyadh's King Khalid resumed limited operations 17 October after the 10 October strike that killed 13, but each airline movement still needs GACA permission and the suspension list runs deep: Lufthansa out to 18 Nov, Air France to 7 Nov (and Dubai to 31 Oct), Philippine Airlines to 29 Nov, Qatar Airways RUH and DMM open ended, Cathay's Riyadh and Dubai resumptions pushed to 31 Jan 2027, Delta's Atlanta service to March 2027; Emirates cancelled DXB to RUH on 20 to 21 Oct and flydubai's FZ847/FZ855 are listed to resume Sunday 25 Oct. Oil snapped back on the news: Brent fell to $110.74 at Tuesday's New York open, then recovered to $111.64 early Thursday (03:35 GMT), with Goldman sketching $120 if vessel attacks escalate (AJ ↗ · GB ↗ · F ↗ · FXE ↗).

Why it matters: Dammam is the kingdom's eastern cargo gateway and it sits across the water from the UAE: the war risk map now covers the Gulf coast, not just the Red Sea corridor, and a tug was hit off Khor Fakkan on 18 October. Every Gulf lane quote needs its war risk language current, and the Saudi suspension list (now stretching into 2027 for some carriers) means displaced tonnage and distressed capacity are a structural feature of this peak, not a one week event. The US departure order is the kind of signal that moves insurance markets before it moves schedules.

War Risk · Two Tables, Both Set

Talks continue via Pakistan and Qatar as Trump says he blocked an Israeli strike plan; NYT reports fresh US and Israeli strike options drawn up

Araghchi (21 Oct, FT op-ed): Iran seeks "a just and honourable deal" but is "fully prepared" to fight; Pezeshkian says a plan has been submitted to Washington. The FT reports Trump blocked a multi day Israeli strike plan around 10 Oct to avoid pre midterm escalation, consistent with his no strikes pledge, while the NYT (22 Oct) says US and Israeli planners have renewed strike options including Iran's energy infrastructure. Why it matters: diplomacy and escalation planning are running in parallel, and the market is pricing both (Goldman's $120 and $80 scenarios). Treat any headline from either track as a rate event; nothing about this is resolved before the midterms.

Trade · The Cheese Case Against Section 338

A second lawsuit hits the 50% Canada tariffs as the rare earth clock runs: 19 days to the 10 Nov lapse, no extension published

Gellert Global Group sued in the Court of International Trade (20 Oct) over the Section 338 tariffs, citing over $60,000 in duties on a $120,000 goat cheese shipment and ~$40m a year of exposure; USTR says Canada declined to finalise agreed terms. On rare earths, MOFCOM's 2026 announcements through No. 48 show no extension of the suspension that lapses 10 Nov, and Washington has not extended its suspended affiliates rule either; September China rare earth exports ran 4,269.8t (+6.7% YoY). Separately, US DOT lifted restrictions on new Mexican airline flights (19 Oct).

Why it matters: the courts are now a live variable on the Canada bans, and the rare earth cliff sits 19 days out with the paperwork silent. If the suspension lapses into reinstated licensing, the AI cargo carrying this peak is the first casualty. Watch 10 November like a rate event.
