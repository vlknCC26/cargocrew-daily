The Big Story · Day Seventeen

THE CORRIDOR TALKS COLLAPSE BEFORE THEY START: OMAN POSTPONES, AND BRENT BREAKS $107

The one diplomatic track that could have reopened Hormuz shipping was pulled before it began. Omani FM Badr Albusaidi announced Sunday that today's Salalah meeting between Iran and the Gulf states is postponed "in the interests of consensus", with no new date; Iranian media called it a joint decision "at the request of some regional countries". It came hours after a projectile struck an Iranian cargo vessel near Hengam Island, killing one crewman, the first merchant hull hit in Iranian coastal waters since the tanker campaign resumed, attacker unidentified. Iran had already rejected Oman's July proposal of shared strait control with voluntary fees, demanding compulsory payments and routings closer to Iranian waters; Pezeshkian's line is "Iran won't surrender". The market answered at the Asia open: Brent $107.40, up 2.7%, with the 7m bpd East West pipeline still down and no restart timeline, up roughly 60% on the year. The single hopeful thread holds: no Iranian ballistic fire for a fourth day, the longest quiet since 28 August (AJ ↗ · CNN ↗ · TE ↗).

Why it matters: everything downstream flows from this postponement: the pending IATA fuel print, the surcharge wave behind it, sea to air conversion demand, and Wednesday's Fed, which is ~86% priced to hike into an oil shock. Audit every fixed rate contract and BSA for fuel clause triggers today, pre draft the surcharge advisories, and quote long term only with indexed fuel clauses. Whoever communicates first controls the narrative with forwarders.
