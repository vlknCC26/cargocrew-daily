The Big Story · Day Eighteen

THE NUMBERS FINALLY LAND, AND THEY PRICE DUBAI'S WAR: EXPORTS TO THE US DOWN 19% IN A WEEK

After two silent weeks, both missing benchmarks posted on the same Monday, and together they draw the first hard picture of what the Gulf closure is doing to this market. WorldACD's wk36 (31 Aug to 6 Sep) shows Dubai to USA tonnage down 19% week on week and Dubai to Europe down 61% year on year, even as the wider MESA region holds flat and MESA to USA runs +25% YoY on India (+42%) and Bangladesh (+21%): the traffic is not vanishing, it is rerouting around Dubai onto subcontinent gateways. Global spot printed $3.00/kg with worldwide tonnage down just 1% WoW ("pricing holds steady"). Hours later the IATA fuel print landed at $181.46/bbl, up 6.1% WoW, and that print largely predates Brent's push through $107: next Monday's number carries the rest of the shock. The macro strap: the FOMC opens its two day meeting today with a 25bp hike ~85% priced, the 10 year at 5.00%, and the decision landing tomorrow ~18:00 UTC (WACD ↗ · IATA ↗ · Kip ↗).

Why it matters: the benchmarks are back, and they say two things at once: your home hub's export lanes are bleeding share to India and Bangladesh, and fuel costs just jumped 6% with another leg already loaded. The commercial answers are concrete: issue the surcharge advisories today with the real $181.46 number attached, pitch subcontinent to US uplift through partner metal while the +42% India wave runs, and defend the Dubai transshipment story with DWC capacity certainty rather than price. Do not let a competitor narrate your own market's data to your customers first.
