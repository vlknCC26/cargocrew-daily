The Big Story · Day Nine

THE TANKER WAR GOES KINETIC: US SINKS FIVE, IRAN FIRES TWENTY, BRENT KISSES $100

The ladder jumped two rungs overnight. CENTCOM confirmed destroying five IRGC linked oil tankers (four in the Gulf of Oman, one near Kharg Island) after failed Iranian missile attacks on a US warship across two days; Rubio's formulation is now declared policy: every attack on US ships and "they're going to lose tankers". Iran answered with roughly 20 ballistic missiles at the Al Azraq base in Jordan and two US destroyers; Jordan intercepted 18, none caused casualties. The IRGC Navy then told tanker crews near Kuwait and Bahrain ports to evacuate immediately, a direct threat to Gulf feeder logistics, while the Houthis wounded 73 in strikes on Saudi oil facilities, the worst exchange since the July truce collapse. Treasury layered on 36 new sanctions designations, including airlines. The market's verdict: Brent $99.49 overnight, a fourth straight session up, roughly 25% higher since early August, with the next IATA fuel print due Monday off an already record $171.01. The Oman corridor stays "close" but unopened (CBS ↗ · Reuters ↗).

Why it matters: the IRGC evacuation warning at Kuwait and Bahrain is the first explicit threat to named Gulf port zones; if feeder shipping there stalls, urgent cargo converts to air within days and DXB and DWC are the natural gateways. Hold the 72 hour validity discipline, keep war risk clauses on every charter and BSA, and treat Monday's fuel print (which will capture the $100 run) as already priced into anything you quote past the weekend.

Trade · Section 338

Washington answers Canada within 24 hours: import bans and a $50bn procurement purge

Five proclamations under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930: bans on certain Canadian alcohol, dairy and vehicle related goods from 29 Sept, expanded tariff lists from 15 Sept, and Canadian products stripped from federal procurement.

Why it matters: the whipsaw scenario is live. Transborder demand faces bans, not just tariffs; Canadian buyers re source harder toward Asia and the Gulf, and Dubai consolidated uplift into YYZ and YUL gets more attractive by the week.
