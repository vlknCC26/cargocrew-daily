The Big Story · Home Market in the Crosshairs

WASHINGTON SQUEEZES DUBAI'S BANKING CHANNELS AS HORMUZ REOPENING RUMORS SWIRL

Friday's OFAC action was smaller than the feared big bank designation, but it landed squarely in CargoCrew's home market: Treasury named Reza Mohammad Taeedi, the Dubai based manager of Bank Melli's Dubai branch, plus a Hong Kong front company, both tagged for secondary sanctions under Operation Economic Outcast, explicitly targeting Iran's access to UAE banking channels. A parallel FinCEN move proposes cutting Banque Misr UAE's five branches off from US correspondent accounts (unverified pending Treasury's own page). Meanwhile Reuters reports rumors of a weekend deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz: only 7 vessels transited Friday against 17 Thursday, and Brent closed at $89.31, down 5% on the week despite everything (OFAC ↗ · Reuters ↗).

Why it matters: two forces pulling opposite directions on Dubai this weekend. The compliance screw tightens: every forwarder, GSA and carrier treasury team in the UAE should be rechecking counterparties before Monday. And the volume story could flip: if a Hormuz reopening deal lands, the boxes that fled to Colombo and Nhava Sheva start planning their way back, and the first mover on a reopening product wins. Prepare both versions now.
