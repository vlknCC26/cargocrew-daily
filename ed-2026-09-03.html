The Big Story · Day Three

THE MARKETS EXHALE, THE RAMP DOES NOT: NO FOURTH STRIKE WAVE, BUT ETIHAD DELAYS 14 ROUTES AND EMIRATES CANCELS EK206

Wednesday brought a pause in the shooting and a mess on the ground. No new US strike wave followed Tuesday's roughly 100 target barrage, and Trump said he is "prepared to strike Iran at any time" but does not expect a long conflict. Saudi Arabia, Qatar and the UAE jointly condemned Iran's attack on the tanker Sidr, in which two Filipino crew were killed, and the UAE called the strikes on its neighbours a flagrant violation of sovereignty. Markets read de-escalation: Brent held flat at $95.5, gold eased to $4,403, the 10 year slipped to 4.78% and September hike odds fell to about 61% on a weak ADP jobs print. The airports read something else. Etihad delayed 14 or more routes, Emirates cancelled Thursday's EK206 to New York, flydubai trimmed rotations to Moscow, Istanbul and South Asia, and Bahrain, Kuwait and Amman were all disrupted; about 40 ships transited Hormuz on 1 September under US Navy guidance (National ↗ · AJ ↗).

Why it matters: the gap between the two stories is the sales pitch. Financial markets are pricing calm while Gulf belly capacity absorbs delay cascades, and that spread lands squarely on freighter uplift. Customers whose cargo sat through Wednesday's delays will take calls today: offer guaranteed uplift on freighters that do not depend on passenger banks, and get it signed before the overdue BAI print and WorldACD wk35 reprice the market.
