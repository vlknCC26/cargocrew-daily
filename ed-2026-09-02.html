The Big Story · The Tanker War Restarts

TWO TANKERS HIT NEAR OMAN, US STRIKES IRGC MINE LAYERS AND RADAR, IRAN ANSWERS AT JORDAN AND BAHRAIN: BRENT NEARS $95

Tuesday brought the clearest re-ignition of the shipping war since the ceasefire. After projectiles struck the tankers Senegal Prosperity and Sidr near Oman on Monday evening, CENTCOM launched a fresh wave of strikes on IRGC air defence sites, radar, maritime assets, mine laying capabilities and communications. Iran answered with missiles at Jordan (10 of 13 intercepted) and claimed drone attacks on Bahrain, a widening of the target set. Brent rose about 5% toward $95, its highest since late July, and was quoted near $96.7 overnight. The 10 year hit 4.80%, a 20 month high, and September hike odds firmed to 66 to 70%. Through it all DXB and DWC stayed open, with Emirates and flydubai reportedly holding schedules while Etihad and Qatar Airways faced cancellations (single source) (CBS ↗ · TE ↗).

Why it matters: this is the whole commercial picture in one story. Fuel surcharges will follow Brent within days, MESA capacity is already 11% below pre war (the Gulf 17% below), and IATA's July lanes show Europe to ME down 16%. The sales message is resilience: CargoCrew's carriers are flying while others cancel. Lock blocked space and BSAs today, before the next surcharge round, and sell schedule reliability as the product.
