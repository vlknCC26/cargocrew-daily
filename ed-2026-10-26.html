The Big Story · Reopening Under Fire

RIYADH REOPENS AND THE MISSILES FOLLOW: TWELVE INTERCEPTS ON RESUMPTION DAY, SAREE DECLARES KKIA A STANDING MILITARY TARGET, AND THE CARRIERS ANSWER WITH NOVEMBER, DECEMBER AND EVEN 2027

King Khalid International partially resumed Sunday after a two week closure, T1 for domestic and T2 for Saudi national carriers, and Saudia and Flynas did operate; but AFP, citing Flightradar24, counted "a small number of flights departed and most were canceled", with GACA still clearing movements flight by flight (SBS, 26 Oct). The Houthis marked the day with their heaviest barrage in a week: the coalition said it intercepted 12 ballistic missiles and two drones Sunday over Riyadh, Khamis Mushait and the southern region (CGTN tally; al-Maliki), with two people injured by falling shrapnel and three residential buildings damaged. Spokesman Yahya Saree claimed three attack waves including one on KKIA itself that "successfully achieved its objective" (contradicted by the intercept record, UNVERIFIED), renewed his designation of the airport as a military target, and warned aircraft out of Saudi airspace except over Mecca and Medina; the Houthi Saba agency separately told civilians to stay clear of Saudi oil facilities. The airlines are writing their own verdict on the reopening: Qatar Airways finally broke its silence (Riyadh suspended up to and including 25 Oct, Dammam suspended with no date, Jeddah cut from seven to four daily), Air France extended Riyadh to 14 Nov, Lufthansa runs to 18 Nov (Riyadh) and 30 Nov (Dammam), Akasa to 30 Nov, Philippine Airlines to 29 Nov, Scoot to 2 Dec, and Cathay Pacific has written Riyadh off to 31 January 2027; Delta's Atlanta launch moved to March 2027 (The National, 25 Oct). Even the Dakar Rally is leaving Saudi Arabia for 2027. In Washington, Trump ruled out the weekend strike his own ambassador had floated: "There will not be a strike", while the WSJ reports the USS George Washington is being rearmed in the Arabian Sea with standoff missiles and bunker busters (SBS ↗ · National ↗ · AA ↗).

Why it matters: a reopening that gets attacked the same day is not a reopening, it is a test shot, and the carriers scored it: the displacement calendar has stopped being a November story and now runs to December (Scoot), January (Cathay) and March (Delta). Every week the big names add to their suspension dates is a week the DWC transshipment and RFS argument writes itself; sell against the carriers' own published boards, not against the official reopening. And Saree's explicit airspace warning, everywhere except the holy cities, is the sentence that keeps war risk premiums on every Saudi lane whatever KKIA's departure screen shows.

Trump rules out the Sunday strike his ambassador floated, while the George Washington is rearmed with bunker busters and JMIC counts nearly 500 ships waiting at Hormuz

Section 338 gets its first product list rewrite effective 30 Oct, the Kremlin floats a Trump, Xi AND Putin meeting at Shenzhen, and the rare earth lapse is now 15 days out

The EU €2 parcel fee starts Saturday 1 Nov, VAT due on the fee itself, alongside a new product ID requirement. Nothing new on soybeans or port fees.

Why it matters: the 30 Oct list change is four days out and immediately actionable: any customer flying Canadian machinery parts, valves or carpets needs reclassification advice this week. A trilateral summit framing makes Shenzhen bigger and the rare earth file smaller, which keeps the 10 Nov lapse as base case, and AI airfreight repricing on a licensing snapback stays the tail risk to position for.
