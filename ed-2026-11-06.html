The Big Story · The Peace Trade Lasted One Day

THE PEACE TRADE UNWOUND IN ONE SESSION: BRENT REBOUNDED 3.9% TO $98.29 ON IRANIAN CLAIMS OF FIVE BURNING TANKERS, THE DOW GAVE BACK 1,833 POINTS, THE NASDAQ CLOSED IN CORRECTION WITH CHIP STOCKS DOWN 12%, AND DECEMBER HIKE ODDS FELL TO TWO IN THREE, ALL WHILE IRAN REPORTEDLY PUT A NUCLEAR PROPOSAL IN FRONT OF WASHINGTON AND TRUMP THREATENED TO "DESTROY THE REST OF THE COUNTRY"

Yesterday's risk warning No. 1 (a 17% crude collapse with no signed peace) cashed within 24 hours, in both directions at once. Brent settled up $3.67 (+3.88%) at $98.29 and WTI up 2.72% at $90.52 Thursday, with Friday early prints near $99.5 and $91.3, as the expanded Iranian tanker claims and a projectile strike on an unidentified tanker 50nm north of Qatar revived the threat premium; the week still shapes as Brent down over 15% (Reuters via Investing.com, 6 Nov; Kitco, 5 Nov). Equities travelled the other way, harder: the Dow fell 1,833 points (3.44%) to 51,523, the S&P 500 dropped 4.36% to 7,354, its steepest day since April 2025, and the Nasdaq lost 5.52% to close in correction, led by a 12.25% collapse in the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (Nvidia down 9.3%, AMD down 17.3%) on profit taking plus fear that the new Democratic Congress brings tighter AI infrastructure oversight; the VIX jumped about 65%, its biggest one day spike in over two years, and retail investors net sold $358m, the heaviest since 2020 (Yahoo Finance wrap, 5 Nov). The diplomacy, meanwhile, got stranger and arguably bigger: Iran has reportedly sent a nuclear proposal to the US through Qatari mediators and Washington is reviewing it (Al Jazeera live, 6 Nov), Araghchi phoned FMs across the Gulf including Saudi's bin Farhan and the UAE's Sheikh Abdullah, and Oman and Iran are discussing "maritime understandings" to reopen Hormuz, while Trump, after a first post midterm Iran meeting, warned the US "may have to destroy the rest of the country", claimed Iran needs 25 to 30 years to rebuild, and acknowledged 18 US service members killed (AJ ↗ · GN ↗ · Kitco ↗).

Why it matters: the desk shipped the reopening brief into the top of the peace trade, and the 24 hour quote validity and both ways scenario framing it carried are exactly what Thursday vindicated. Nothing in the brief is wrong, but its probability weights moved: crude is back near $100, the threat tape is expanding (five tankers plus a tug claimed, still unverified), and the chip complex that anchors the AI cargo vertical just had its worst day in years without a single cargo demand datapoint turning down (AF KLM, DHL and Emirates all printed cargo strength THIS WEEK). Hold the two script discipline: fuel and war risk quoted short, AI vertical demand argued from carrier revenue, not from share prices.
