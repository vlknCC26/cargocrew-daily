The Big Story · The Off Ramp Goes Missing

"THE ERA OF PROPORTIONATE RESPONSES IS OVER": TEHRAN ESCALATES ITS LANGUAGE AS BRENT GAPS TOWARD $97

Day seven opens the week with the ladder intact and the off ramp missing. Iran's chief negotiator Ghalibaf declared Sunday that future responses will be "faster, heavier and more painful" and called Saturday's strikes "only a start"; the IRGC released video of explosives laden projectiles hitting ships in the Strait of Hormuz and repeated its disputed claim of damaging a US carrier. CENTCOM confirmed that of the three tankers it struck, two are permanently disabled and one sank. Tehran stood up an "economic war headquarters", threatened "restricted maritime zones" near Hormuz, and Bessent noted about 30 million barrels of Iranian crude sit unsold. The market's answer: Brent gapped up toward $97 at the Asia open, extending the strongest week since July at +8%, while Asian equities outside India shrugged (Nikkei +2.2%, Kospi +3.0%). EASA's Gulf overwater warning now runs to 30 September (AJ ↗ · ABC ↗ · TE ↗).

Why it matters: the squeeze is now two sided: Xeneta has ME inbound spots still 20 to 100% above pre conflict just as the fuel line explodes, and the IATA monitor's violent catch up print lands today. Every quote issued this week needs a shorter validity (48 to 72 hours) and a bigger fuel buffer, plus a Hormuz war risk escalation clause on anything charter or BSA shaped. Reprice this morning, not after the print.
