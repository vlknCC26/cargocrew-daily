The Big Story · Diplomacy Under Fire

THE SAUDI DEFENCE MINISTER SITS DOWN WITH IRAN'S PRESIDENT IN TEHRAN WHILE HOUTHI MISSILES ARC TOWARD RIYADH AND, FOR THE FIRST TIME, THE EASTERN REGION: THE WAR'S HIGHEST LEVEL CONTACT OPENS WITH THE AIRPORTS STILL UNDER FIRE

Prince Khalid bin Salman flew to Tehran Monday and met President Masoud Pezeshkian, leading a Makkah Joint Defense Agreement delegation alongside Turkish intelligence chief Ibrahim Kalin and Pakistani army chief Asim Munir, with SNSC secretary Mohsen Rezaei across the table; Riyadh framed the visit as support for the Qatari and Pakistani mediation and for opening "channels of negotiation to prevent a renewed military escalation" (Arab News, 26 Oct). Rezaei called the talks "very good" and said Iran could shorten its seven day implementation period under the Mecca framework to five or four days; Bagheri Kani countered with seven conditions Washington must meet before Tehran negotiates, and Oman's foreign minister floated a non-aggression pact spanning the GCC six plus Iraq and Iran (Gulf News live, 27 Oct). The guns did not pause for the diplomats: hours after the KKIA reopening, a ballistic missile was fired at the airport and intercepted, GACA responded with NOTAMs requiring prior approval for every arrival and departure and barring KKIA as a diversion field, and overnight into Tuesday the coalition destroyed three ballistic missiles over the Eastern Region, a new vector toward Dammam and the oil coast, plus seven drones over Riyadh and the south. Cirium counts 86% of KKIA flights cancelled since 8 Oct (4,071 of 4,736) and 45% of all Saudi departures 8 to 25 Oct. Pezeshkian flies on to Moscow today for the gas exporters' summit and a Putin meeting, while CNN reports US forces could strike Iran within 96 hours of a Trump order, none yet given (Gulf News ↗ · AGN ↗ · Al-Monitor ↗).

Why it matters: KBS in Tehran is the single most hopeful headline of the war so far, and Brent eased on it, but the overnight Eastern Region vector is the hedge the market put against it: missiles toward Dammam and the oil coast while the ministers talked. For pricing, nothing de-escalates until a mechanism is announced; for planning, a real Mecca framework with a four to five day implementation clock means a Saudi reopening could arrive FAST once signed, and the backlog surge with it. Run both scenarios: war premium quotes with short validity today, and a reopening surge playbook on the shelf for the day a deal prints.
