HORMUZ DOWN 20%, A FRESH HIT, BRENT AT $95: THE SQUEEZE TIGHTENS ON EVERY FRONT

One week after the 13 August drone strikes on two UAE tankers, confirmed Hormuz crossings fell to 95 for the week, a near 20% drop, with just three vessels crossing on 16 August; the US military is convoying 15 to 20 tankers nightly through a southern corridor. On 18 August an unidentified projectile struck an outbound vessel, damaging the engine room and causing a crew casualty (tracker ↗, counts unverified). Brent touched $95.40, up 41.9% YoY (Fortune ↗). And the promised Bessent "economic isolation" package slipped its window: OFAC's 20 August action names only three IRGC linked Turkish nationals, no oil, shipping or banking measures (OFAC ↗).

Why it matters: the squeeze is compounding, not resolving: less sea capacity, dearer fuel, and a sanctions shock still loaded. This is what is holding BAI +19.2% YoY and MESA yields +48% YoY. Keep same day Gulf charter quoting on, and price fuel escalation into anything longer than 30 days.
